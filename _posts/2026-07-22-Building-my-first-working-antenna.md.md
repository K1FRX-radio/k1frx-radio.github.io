# Building my first _working_ 8antenna

I'm super pumped about building my own antennas. There's something magical about it: having the ability of expertly cutting a length of wire, and verifying that it resonates at the correct frequency feels so good, so niche. There has to be only a very small group of people that cares about this, in the whole world! So I'm very interested in building my skills to earn my place amongst these dark wizards.

The very first thing I ever tried was a dual band Yagi antenna to contact the space station, but man that was _not_ the right starter project.

There are too many little details and nuances in ddition to having just basic making and hacking skills: materials, connectors, sizes, lengths, you name it. Every single detail plays a critical role on ending up with an antenna that will work _at all_, and even more in making it work _well_.

So I decided to put that Yagi on the back burner for a moment and focus on something simpler. I looked at a few different designs, and landed on a center-fed dipole for the 2m band. It is simple, compact yet forgiving enough, and requires a bit more work and diligence than a simple monopole, so I got to work.

As always, my mind went a little bit too far too fast, and got some aluminum rods, but after a bit of thinking I decided to pull back the scope and start with an even simpler version, made with two lengths of electrical wire.

I printed a "hub" to hold a panel mounted bnc connector and to provide some fastening and stress relief to the wires. Also printed two little clamps to be able to hang the antenna.
